Mind the Build: Why Published BMv2 Throughput Figures Cannot Be Compared

Chi-Yen Fan

 National Cheng Kung University
 TainanTaiwan

The software P4 switch bmv2 anchors much of the P4 research pipeline, and papers
routinely report its forwarding performance in Mbps. We surveyed 18 papers that
measure or relay bmv2 throughput: reported figures span 2,500x
(0.57 Mbps to 1.4 Gbps), none reports build flags or provides enough
information to reconstruct the binary, and two peer-reviewed papers order bmv2
versus Open vSwitch in opposite directions. We then quantified, under
preregistered protocols on one machine, three variables that no surveyed paper
isolates. (1) Build: the officially recommended compile configuration
versus a default-style build is 12x on a three-hop production path and
R=8.0 (quantisation interval 5.14--12.0) isolated to a single hop with the
control plane live --- so part of the production-path ratio is not the compiler
flags. (2) Unit: across 64/256/1024 B frames, clean-rate pps varies
1.25x while the same data span 16.0x in Mbit/s --- Mbps without a
frame size is a 16x ambiguity. (3) Flows: per-flow highest clean
rate falls monotonically with flow count, replicated, while OvS at matched
working points does not. In two of our three experiments, our own instrument's
limit nearly became the reported number; none of the 18 papers states what
limited its measurement.

A 2,500x spread nobody can arbitrate

Of 18 surveyed papers that measure or relay bmv2 performance, 12 measure it
directly. Among those twelve: 0/12 report compiler flags or
optimisation level, 0/12 run any build comparison, 3/12 name the
variant (all one lab's lineage), 1/12 names a version. Their throughput
figures span 2,500x, from 0.57 Mbps at 64 B
 to 1.4 Gbps on physical NICs .
With the information the papers themselves provide, no two of these numbers can
be shown to measure the same artifact. We say mutually incomparable
deliberately --- we did not attempt reproductions, so we claim nothing about
reproducibility.

The consequence is concrete: one line of work measures Open vSwitch two orders
of magnitude above bmv2 , while another
concludes the P4 pipeline outperforms OvS (a
reactive-control-plane effect, per its own analysis) --- and a reader cannot
arbitrate, because neither reports its build; even one lab's adjacent papers
differ 1.7x per switch with no build stated
.

The missing variable is not obscure. The bmv2 repository's README warns that
build flags "can have a massive impact on performance"
, and its performance documentation lists the recommended
configuration; two of the surveyed papers cite that very document and still do
not report their own build. The knowledge exists in project documentation and
forums; the reporting norm does not exist in the 18 papers. The adjacent
NFV benchmarking community shows the norm is attainable: its flagship study
pins per-switch commits, build notes, and parameters
. Our study is a P4-community instance of a
familiar genre: an unreported, "trivial" variable large enough to invalidate
published comparisons .

Three preregistered measurements

All experiments preregistered outcome intervals and the meaning of every
outcome before data; the replication unit is the interleaved arm (fresh fabric
per arm); ladders are x1.5 (20\% per rung); the running binary is
identified by symbol signature against a negative control, never by PATH; CPU
gates use per-process attribution, trusted only after a positive control made
them fire. Every arm reads both endpoint counters and paired
ingress-RX/egress-TX interface counters --- kernel drop counters read zero
even where bmv2 lost 79.66\% internally. Raw data is content-hash archived.

(1) Build. Stock is a default-style build (-O0, logging
compiled in, as installed by common guides); fast is the officially
recommended composite configuration (-O3 --disable-logging-macros
--disable-elogger). On our three-hop production path the UDP zero-loss point
differs 12x. Isolated to a single switch --- one hop, control plane
still live --- it is 45 vs. 360 Mbit/s: R=8.0, zero spread across arms,
quantisation interval (5.14,12.0), knee-shape interpolation 7.8.
This landed in the preregistered outcome "part of the production-path ratio is
not the compiler flags." We report both working points; whether the
difference is path length or the live control plane is preregistered future
work, and this abstract attributes it to neither.

(2) Unit. Sweeping the standard NFV frame-size axis
, clean-rate pps is near-constant while the same
cells span 16.0x as bit rate (Fig. ); the registered
ratios P(256)/P(64)=1.25 and P(1024)/P(64)=1.00 fall in the
pps-ceiling interval and far from the bps-ceiling prediction (0.15--0.40 /
0.03--0.12). A registered sender-side control passed with 9.6x headroom,
so the flat line is bmv2's, not the generator's; all six arms also truncated at
the same pps rung under a rule that is blind to frame size. Reporting
bmv2 capacity in Mbps without the frame size is therefore not imprecision; it
is a 16x ambiguity. All 18 surveyed papers report Mbps (one adds a
single pps point ); none sweeps frame size for bmv2 ---
one paper's own two sizes imply near-constant pps, unanalysed in its text
.

(3) Flows. With all n flows pinned to one path class (so
flows/switch = flows/link =n by construction), the per-flow highest clean
rate falls monotonically in each of two independent arms, cells agreeing
within one ladder rung (Fig. ). The aggregate is an arithmetic
consequence; we report
no ratio --- an earlier "collapse factor" divided a clean threshold by a
saturation knee, two different quantities, and was withdrawn. OvS at matched
working points showed no decline (earlier measurement; a same-ladder control
is planned).

What limited our measurements

Each experiment's dominant threat turned out to be the same: our own
instrument's ceiling masquerading as the system's. It happened twice --- a
flow-count ladder's top rung was mistaken for the switch's single-flow ceiling
long enough to enter a draft, and the first build-ratio round's ladder ended
below the fast build's ability, censoring the numerator --- and was blocked
once, by the registered sender gate. Both slips were caught by rule-driven
ladder tops and post-hoc checks, and both corrections moved away from
the more publishable answer. None of the 18 surveyed papers states what
limited its measurement; we read this as the same disease as
Mbps-without-size, in a second form.

Takeaway

We propose a four-line provenance minimum for any software-switch number:
build (flags/commit), unit (pps with the frame size),
placement (flows per switch and link), and limit (what
stopped the measurement --- the system, or the instrument). Preregistrations,
raw data (content-hashed), and scripts will be released.
